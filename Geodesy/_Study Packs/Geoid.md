# 🌊 Geoid

The **geoid** is the equipotential surface of Earth's gravity field that coincides, on average, with **mean sea level**. It is the "true" shape of the Earth — irregular, because mass is unevenly distributed inside the planet.

## Why it matters
- Heights measured above the geoid are **orthometric heights (H)** — what spirit levels and tide gauges actually measure.
- The geoid is the reference for [[Orthometric Height]] and the bridge between [[GNSS]] ellipsoidal heights and practical surveying heights.

## Key relationship
```
h = H + N
```
- h = [[Ellipsoidal Height]] (above the [[Reference Ellipsoid]], from GPS)
- H = [[Orthometric Height]] (above the geoid)
- N = [[Geoid Undulation]] (geoid vs ellipsoid)

## Related
- Defined by the [[Gravity Field]] — irregular due to mass anomalies.
- Models: EGM96, EGM2008, GEOID12B (provide N at any φ,λ).
- Contrast with the smooth mathematical [[Reference Ellipsoid]].

## Orthometric Height
# 📉 Orthometric Height (H)

**Orthometric height H** is the height above the [[Geoid]] — what spirit levels and tide gauges measure. This is the "real" height used in mapping, civil engineering, and cadastre.

## Relationship
```
h = H + N
```
- h = [[Ellipsoidal Height]] (from GPS)
- N = [[Geoid Undulation]] (from a geoid model: EGM96 / EGM2008 / GEOID12B)

So a GNSS survey gives h; subtract N to get usable H.

## GNSS
# 🛰️ GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System)

**GNSS** is the family of satellite positioning systems (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BDS). It delivers [[Geodetic Coordinates]] + [[Ellipsoidal Height]] anywhere with sky view.

## Signal & error (Semester 4)
- Segments: space / control / user.
- Errors: ionosphere, troposphere, multipath, clock — mitigated by **DOP** awareness, **RTK** (real-time kinematic), and **PPP** (precise point positioning).

## Precision techniques
- **RTK** — cm-level via a base station / network (outputs in [[Local ENU NEU]]).
- **PPP** — cm-level via precise orbits/clocks ([[IGS]], [[IERS]]).
- High-precision frames tie back to [[ITRF]] / [[WGS84]].

## Ellipsoidal Height
# 📈 Ellipsoidal Height (h)

**Ellipsoidal height h** is the distance above the [[Reference Ellipsoid]] along its normal. It is what [[GNSS]] / [[GPS]] measures **directly**.

## The height trio (same point, very different numbers)
| Sym | Name | Reference | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| h | Ellipsoidal height | [[Reference Ellipsoid]] | GNSS/GPS directly |
| N | [[Geoid Undulation]] | Geoid vs ellipsoid | Geoid model output |
| H | [[Orthometric Height]] | [[Geoid]] | Leveling, mapping, civil eng. |

```
h = H + N
```

## Reference Ellipsoid
# 🥚 Reference Ellipsoid

A **reference ellipsoid** is a smooth mathematical surface that *approximates* the [[Geoid]]. Earth is not a sphere — it is flattened at the poles and bulged at the equator — so we model it with a biaxial ellipsoid defined by two parameters.

## Defining parameters (WGS84 example)
- **a** — semimajor axis (equatorial radius) ≈ 6378.137 km
- **b** — semiminor axis (polar radius) ≈ 6356.752 km
- **f** — flattening = (a − b)/a ≈ 1/298.257
- **e** — first eccentricity = √(1 − b²/a²) ≈ 0.0818
- **e′** — second eccentricity = √(a²/b² − 1) ≈ 0.0821

See [[Flattening]] for why f is small but critical.

## Role
- Basis for [[Geodetic Coordinates]] (φ, λ, h).
- Sits inside a [[Datum]] — the ellipsoid alone means nothing until positioned relative to Earth.
- The geoid deviates from the ellipsoid by the [[Geoid Undulation]] N (meters).

## Common ellipsoids
- WGS84 → [[WGS84]] / [[GPS]]
- GRS80 → [[GRS80]] / [[NAD83]] / [[ETRS89]]

## Geoid Undulation
# 🌊 Geoid Undulation (N)

**Geoid undulation N** is the separation between the [[Geoid]] and the [[Reference Ellipsoid]] at a point. It varies by tens of meters globally.

## Role in the height equation
```
h = H + N
```
- h = [[Ellipsoidal Height]] (GPS)
- H = [[Orthometric Height]]
- N = geoid undulation (the converter)

## How we get N
- Global/regional geoid models: **EGM96, EGM2008, GEOID12B**.
- At a given (φ, λ): bilinear interpolation in a geoid grid file.

## Gravity Field
# 🌀 Gravity Field

The **gravity field** of Earth is the combined effect of gravitation and centrifugal force from rotation. It defines the direction of "down" everywhere and shapes the [[Geoid]].

## Why it matters in geodesy
- The [[Geoid]] is an **equipotential surface** of this field — that is why mean sea level is bumpy, not a perfect ellipsoid.
- [[Physical Geodesy]] (Semester 6) studies geoid undulations via Stokes' formula and global models (EGM96, EGM2008).
- Gravity anomalies let us recover the geoid from [[Ellipsoidal Height]] + [[Orthometric Height]].

## Basic Geodesy
# 📄 Basic Geodesy (source note)

Extracted from `geodesy_basics.pdf` (8.8 KB) by AIGIS. A short, practical English primer covering the essentials of geodesy.

## What it covers
1. **Shape of the Earth** — flattened sphere; reference ellipsoid defined by semimajor axis *a*, semiminor axis *b*, flattening *f*, eccentricities *e*, *e′*.
2. **Geoid** — the "true" equipotential surface ≈ mean sea level; [[Orthometric Height]] H measured above it.
3. **Datums** — horizontal reference frames: [[NAD27]], [[NAD83]], [[WGS84]], [[ETRS89]], GDA94/GDA2020.
4. **Coordinate systems** — [[Geodetic Coordinates]] (φ, λ, h), [[Geocentric Cartesian ECEF]] (X,Y,Z), [[Projected Coordinates]] (UTM, State Plane, Web Mercator), [[Local ENU NEU]].
5. **Heights & vertical datums** — h, N, H with **h = H + N**.
6. **Map projections** — conformal / equal-area / equidistant / azimuthal trade-offs.
7. **Common computations** — [[Vincenty Formula]], ECEF conversion, [[Datum Transformation]] (Helmert 7-param), UTM forward/inverse, geoid lookup, projection↔geographic.
8. **Tools** — [[PROJ]], [[GeographicLib]], GDAL/OGR, national agencies (NGS, etc.).

## Key constants (WGS84)
- a = 6378.137 km (equatorial radius)
- b = 6356.752 km (polar radius)
- f = 1/298.257
- e = 0.0818, e′ = 0.0821
