# Plate Tectonics

## For Geodesy & Earth Science

**Core Idea:** Plate tectonics describes the movement of Earth's lithospheric plates. GNSS measurements directly measure plate motions, making geodesy essential for monitoring plate velocities, strain accumulation, and earthquake hazard.

---

## Key Plates

| Plate | Velocity (mm/yr) | Direction |
|-------|-------------------|-----------|
| Pacific | ~70 | NW |
| Nazca | ~65 | E (subducting) |
| Indo-Australian | ~60 | N (into Eurasia) |
| North American | ~25 | W |
| Eurasian | ~20 | E |
| Philippine Sea | ~60 | NW |

## In Geodesy Context

**Plate motion at GNSS stations:**

- GPS-derived plate velocities: 1-2 mm/yr accuracy

- Indonesia: convergence of Indo-Australian plate with Eurasia (~60 mm/yr)

- Subduction zones: strain accumulation → earthquakes

## Plate Boundaries

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Divergent | Plates move apart | Mid-Atlantic Ridge |
| Convergent | Plates collide | Java Trench (subduction) |
| Transform | Plates slide past | San Andreas Fault |
