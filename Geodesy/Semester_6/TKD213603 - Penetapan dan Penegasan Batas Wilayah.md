## 1. Overview

Boundary demarcation (*penetapan dan penegasan batas wilayah*) is the systematic process of establishing the geographic limits of administrative, territorial, or jurisdictional areas. In Indonesia, this involves precise legal, technical, and procedural frameworks to define and record boundaries between provinces, districts, villages, and other administrative units.

---

## 2. Legal Framework for Boundary Determination

### 2.1 Constitutional and Statutory Basis

| Law | Year | Coverage |
|------|------|----------|
| **UUD 1945** (Constitution) | 1945 | Ultimate sovereign authority over territory |
| **UU No. 5/1960** (UUPA) | 1960 | Land rights, boundaries between private and state |
| **PP 24/1997** | 1997 | Technical standards for boundary determination |
| **PP 47/1999** | 1999 | Administration of territorial regions |
| **Permendagri No. 45/2016** | 2016 | Technical guidelines for boundary demarcation |

### 2.2 Principle of Utmost Effect (*Pasti Maksimal*)

The principle that boundaries should be defined by visible, permanent natural features or officially demarcated lines:

- Rivers (*sungai*) — middle line, with consideration of meanders

- Mountains (*gunung*) — high points (summits)

- Ridges (*punguk*) — outline lines

- Lakes (*danau*) — outline, with islands

- Roads (*jalan*) — center line if official boundary

- Streams (*krukut*) — middle line unless juridically defined otherwise

- **Forest boundaries** — regulated by Forest Law No. 41/1999

- **Water boundaries** — regulated by Water Law No. 7/2004

## 4. Boundary Demarcation Process

### 4.1 Pre-Survey Phase

#### 4.1.1 Initiation and Planning

1. **Request submission** (*pengajuan permintaan*) - from district/city/municipal offices
2. **Feasibility study** — legal, technical, and resource requirements
3. **Timeline planning** — minimum 3–6 months for complex boundaries
4. **Stakeholder coordination** — include district, provincial, and national agencies

#### 4.1.2 Documentation Review

**Required documents for boundary establishment:**

- **Political basis** — Law No. 32/2004, Government Regulation

- **Legal evidence** — land certificates, village statutes, judicial decisions

- **Technical evidence** — survey maps, GPS coordinates, boundary monuments

- **Juridical evidence** — historical agreements, customary law documentation

### 4.2 Field Survey Phase

#### 4.2.1 Boundary Verification

Verify boundary features through:

**Method 1: Document review and field reconnaissance**
1. Study existing maps and certificates
2. Identify boundary physical features
3. Surveyor's field verification
4. Compare with actual physical situation

**Method 2: Physical demarcation**
1. Establish control points for boundary markers
2. Use GNSS/RTK for precise positioning
3. Mark boundary points with durable markers

#### 4.2.2 Survey Methods

| Method | Equipment | Accuracy | Use |
|--------|-----------|----------|-----|
| **Trigonometric surveying** | Total station, theodolite | ±5 cm horizontal | Large distances, no line-of-sight |
| **Traversing** | EDM + total station | ±2 cm horizontal | Medium distances |
| **GNSS survey** | RTK GNSS | ±2 cm | Urban boundaries |
| **Intersection** | Total station | ±5 cm | Difficult terrain |
| **Resection** | GNSS + total station | ±3 cm | Restricted observation |

### 4.3 Boundary Establishment Methods

#### Method 1: Construction of Boundary Markers

**Types of boundary monuments:**

- **Stele monuments** — permanent concrete markers with identification

- **Peg-towers** — iron towers with steel plates

- **Bronze disks** — underwater for boundary rivers

- **GPS markers** — with RFID chips for digital boundary

**Installation process:**
1. Site preparation — clear vegetation, ensure accessibility
2. Foundation work — concrete footings or deep piles
3. Marker placement — accurate coordinates
4. Documentation — photographs, survey records
5. Public notification — announcement to local authorities and residents

#### Method 2: Legal Boundary Documentation

**Official documents:**

- **Surat Keputusan Penetapan Batas** (Boundary Determination Decree)

- **Berita Acara** (Report of Proceedings)

- **Peta Batas Wilayah** (Boundary Map)

- **Certificate of Boundary Status** (KGB — *Kartu Garis Batas*)

## 5. Indonesian Boundary Law and Policy

### 5.1 Legislation Hierarchy

1. **Indonesian Constitution (1945)** — ultimate authority
2. **National Laws (UU)** — framework legislation
3. **Government Regulations (PP)** — implementing details
4. **Presidential Regulations (Perpres)** — specific programs
5. **Regional Regulations (Perda)** — local implementation

### 5.2 Key Boundary Laws

| Law | Focus | Scope |
|------|-------|-------|
| **Law No. 32/2004** | Regional government system | Provincial, district/city |
| **Law No. 26/2007** | Spatial planning | National spatial plan |
| **Law No. 1/2014** | Maritime boundaries | Territorial waters |
| **Law No. 41/1999** | Forests | Forest area boundaries |
| **Law No. 7/2004** | Waters | Water resource boundaries |

### 5.3 Boundary Dispute Resolution

**Legal mechanisms:**
1. **Negotiation** (*perundingan*) — peaceful settlement
2. **Mediation** (*mediasi*) — third-party facilitation
3. **Arbitration** (*arbitrase*) — legal adjudication
4. **Judicial settlement** (*penegakan hukum*) — court decisions
5. **Administrative settlement** (*penyelesaian administratif*) — government arbitration

**International boundaries:**

- **Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS)** — baseline, territorial sea, EEZ

- **Regional agreements** — ASEAN maritime cooperation

- **Bilateral treaties** — boundary delimitation agreements
