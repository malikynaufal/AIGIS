# FKD211601 — Fisika Lintas Batas

## Course Overview

Interdisciplinary physics — exploring the frontiers where physics intersects with biology, nanotechnology, materials science, and Earth sciences. This course highlights how the fundamental laws of physics govern phenomena across disciplines, from molecular motors to geophysical hazards.

**Contact Hours:** 3 SKS (2 hours lecture + 1 hour seminar per week)
**Prerequisites:** Fisika Klasik, Fisika Dasar II, Mekanika Kuantum
**Co-requisites:** None

---

## 📋 Topics & Outline

### Unit 1: Biophysics (Weeks 1–4)

- **Physics of living systems:** forces at the molecular scale

- **Diffusion and Brownian motion:** Fick's laws, Einstein relation D = kT/(6πμR)

- **Stochastic processes:** random walks, Langevin equation, Fokker-Planck equation

- **Membrane physics:** lipid bilayers, membrane potential

- **Enzyme kinetics** (Michaelis-Menten) from a physical perspective

- **DNA mechanics:** persistence length, elasticity, unwinding energy

- **Molecular motors:** kinesin, ATP synthase — converting chemical energy to mechanical work

- **Medical physics:** MRI (nuclear magnetic resonance), ultrasound imaging

### Unit 2: Nanophysics and Mesoscopic Systems (Weeks 5–8)

- **Nanoscale phenomena:** quantum confinement, surface effects dominate bulk

- **Quantum dots:** energy levels E ∝ 1/L² (particle in a box!)

- **Carbon nanostructures:** fullerenes, carbon nanotubes, graphene
 - Graphene: massless Dirac fermions, 2D electron gas

- **Nanomagnetism:** single-domain vs. multi-domain, superparamagnetism

- **Nano-optics:** plasmonics (surface plasmons on metal nanoparticles)

- **Tunneling at the nanoscale:** STM, SET (single-electron transistor)

- **Self-assembly and molecular nanotechnology**

### Unit 3: Nonlinear Physics and Emergent Phenomena (Weeks 9–12)

- **Nonlinear dynamics:** strange attractors, bifurcations, chaos
 - Lorenz equations: dx/dt = σ(y-x), dy/dt = rx-y-xz, dz/dt = xy-bz

- **Pattern formation:** Bénard convection, Turing patterns

- **Power laws and scale-invariance** in natural systems

- **Self-organized criticality:** sandpile model (Bak, 1987)

- **Fractals:** fractal dimension, Mandelbrot set
 - Natural examples: coastlines, river networks, cloud boundaries

- **Complex systems:** emergence, feedback loops, adaptation

### Unit 4: Applications and Emerging Frontiers (Weeks 13–16)

- **Geophysical applications of interdisciplinary physics:**
 - Earthquake dynamics (spring-block model)
 - Volcanic systems (thermodynamics of magma chambers)
 - Tsunami wave mechanics (shallow water equations)

- **Geophysical remote sensing** from a physical perspective

- **Environmental physics:** climate modeling, energy systems

- **Quantum information** (overview): qubits, entanglement, quantum computing

- **Student presentations** on current frontiers in interdisciplinary physics

- **Final project** or essay on a selected topic

## 🔬 Key Equations

```
Diffusion: ∂C/∂t = D∇²C (Fick's 2nd law)
Einstein relation: D = k_BT/(6πμR)
Langevin eqn: m dv/dt = -γv + F(t) (random force)
Michaelis-Menten: v = V_max[S]/(K_m + [S])
Fractal dimension: N(ε) ∝ ε^{-D_f}
Power law: P(x) = Cx^{-α}
```

## 🎯 Learning Outcomes

Upon completion, students will be able to:
1. Apply statistical and stochastic methods to biological and nanoscale systems
2. Understand quantum confinement effects at the nanoscale
3. Analyze nonlinear dynamics and chaos using phase space methods
4. Recognize emergent phenomena and self-organization in physical systems
5. Connect interdisciplinary physics to real-world applications
6. Critically evaluate interdisciplinary research papers
