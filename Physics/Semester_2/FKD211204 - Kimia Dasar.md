# FKD211204 — Kimia Dasar

## Course Overview

Chemistry for physics students — understanding atomic structure, chemical bonding, and material properties at the molecular and subatomic level. This course bridges physics and chemistry, essential for topics like semiconductor physics, spectroscopy, material science, and quantum mechanics.

**Contact Hours:** 2 SKS (2 hours lecture per week)
**Prerequisites:** Fisika Dasar I
**Co-requisites:** Fisika Dasar II

---

## 📋 Topics & Outline

### Unit 1: Atomic Structure (Weeks 1–5)

- **Historical development:** Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr models

- **Quantum numbers:** n, l, m_l, m_s — describing electron states
  - n = 1,2,3,... (energy/shell)
  - l = 0,1,...,n-1 (shape: s,p,d,f)
  - m_l = -l,...,+l (orientation)
  - m_s = ±½ (spin)

- **Electron configuration** and the Aufbau principle

- **Pauli exclusion principle** — no two electrons share all four quantum numbers

- **Hund's rule** — maximize spin multiplicity in degenerate orbitals

- **Periodic table** as a reflection of electron structure

- Ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity trends

### Unit 2: Chemical Bonding (Weeks 6–10)

- **Ionic bonding:** electron transfer, lattice energy, Born-Landé equation

- **Covalent bonding:** shared electron pairs, VSEPR theory, orbital overlap

- **Metallic bonding:** electron sea model, band theory introduction

- **Coordinate (dative) bonds** in transition metal complexes

- **Bond properties:** bond order, bond length, bond energy

- **Polarity:** electronegativity difference → dipole moment

- **Molecular geometry:** VSEPR shapes (linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, etc.)

- **Intermolecular forces:** van der Waals, hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole

### Unit 3: Molecular Orbital Theory and Band Theory (Weeks 11–14)

- **Molecular orbital (MO) theory:** Hückel approach, bonding/antibonding MOs

- **Bond order** in MO theory

- **Paramagnetism and diamagnetism** explained by MO theory

- From molecules to solids: **formation of energy bands**

- **Band theory of solids:** conductors, semiconductors, insulators
  - Valence band, conduction band, band gap (Eg)
  - Intrinsic vs. extrinsic semiconductors (n-type, p-type)
  - Fermi level and its role in conductivity

- Application: why silicon is the basis of modern electronics

### Unit 4: Materials and Applications (Weeks 15–16)

- **Crystal structures:** simple cubic, BCC, FCC, HCP

- **X-ray diffraction** as a probe of crystal structure (Bragg's law: nλ = 2d sinθ)

- **Thermal properties** of materials: thermal expansion, heat capacity

- **Optical properties:** absorption, emission, photoluminescence

- Relationship between atomic structure and material properties for physics applications

## 🔬 Key Concepts

```
Energy levels:      E_n = -13.6 eV · Z²/n²  (hydrogen)
Bragg's law:        nλ = 2d sinθ
Band gap:           Eg (eV) → determines material class
Fermi-Dirac:        f(E) = 1/(exp((E-EF)/kT) + 1)

Quantum numbers:
  n = 1,2,3,...
  l = 0,...,n-1
  m_l = -l,...,+l
  m_s = ±½
```

---

## 🎯 Learning Outcomes

Upon completion, students will be able to:
1. Explain the quantum mechanical model of the atom using quantum numbers
2. Predict molecular geometry using VSEPR theory
3. Distinguish between ionic, covalent, metallic, and van der Waals bonding
4. Apply band theory to explain conductor/semiconductor/insulator behavior
5. Use Bragg's law for crystal structure determination
6. Connect atomic and molecular structure to macroscopic material properties
