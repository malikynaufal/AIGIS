# FKD211702 — Topik Khusus Fisika

## Course Overview

Special topics in physics — a flexible course that covers advanced or emerging topics based on current research interests of the physics department and industry needs. The course is designed to expose students to the cutting edge of physics beyond standard textbook coverage.

**Contact Hours:** 3 SKS (2 hours lecture + 1 hour seminar per week)
**Prerequisites:** At least 4 semesters of physics coursework
**Co-requisites:** Proyek Akhir I

---

## 📋 Topics & Outline (Rotating Topics)

*Note: The specific topics rotate each year based on faculty expertise and current trends. Below is a representative catalog:*

### Option A: Advanced Photonics and Laser Physics

- Laser physics: gain media, cavity design, Q-switching, mode locking

- Nonlinear optics: second harmonic generation, parametric oscillation

- Fiber optics: propagation modes, dispersion, Erbium-doped fiber amplifiers

- Applications: laser interferometry in geodesy (SLR, LIDAR)

### Option B: Computational Earth Physics

- Earth's interior modeling using seismological data

- Geophysical inverse problems

- Finite element methods for Earth system modeling

- Ocean-atmosphere coupled dynamics

### Option C: Quantum Information and Computing

- Quantum computing: qubits, gates, circuits

- Quantum error correction basics

- Shor's algorithm and Grover's algorithm (overview)

- Quantum simulation of physical systems

- Current state: IBM Qiskit, Google Sycamore, quantum supremacy

### Option D: Advanced Sensors and Metrology

- MEMS and NEMS (micro/nano electromechanical systems)

- Quantum sensors: atomic interferometers, SQUIDs, NV centers

- Ultra-precision measurement: gravitational wave detectors (LIGO)

- Calibration and traceability in national metrology

### Option E: Data Science for Physics

- Machine learning applications in physics

- Neural networks for pattern recognition in experimental data

- Bayesian inference in physics

- Big data challenges in physics (LHC, cosmology surveys)

## 📋 Structure (Semester Outline)

### Weeks 1–3: Introduction and Foundations

- Overview of the specific topic

- Historical context and motivation

- Key theoretical framework

### Weeks 4–8: Core Theory

- Detailed mathematical/physical framework

- Problem sets and derivation exercises

- Reading assignments from primary literature

### Weeks 9–12: Modern Applications

- Case studies from current research

- Guest lectures from industry or visiting researchers

- Critical analysis of recent publications

### Weeks 13–16: Student-Led Learning

- Student presentations on subtopics

- Group projects applying the course material

- Connections to capstone project (Proyek Akhir I/II)

---

## 🎯 Learning Outcomes

Upon completion, students will be able to:
1. Understand the current state of research in the chosen special topic
2. Read and critically analyze advanced physics papers
3. Apply specialized knowledge to their capstone project
4. Present complex physics topics clearly to peers
5. Identify potential career directions in the special topic area
6. Connect specialized physics to broader scientific and engineering challenges
