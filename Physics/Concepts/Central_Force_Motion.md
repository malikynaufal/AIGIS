# Central Force Motion

> Kepler problem, effective potential, orbits, Bertrand's theorem

> **Part of:** [[Physics MOC]] · [[Physics_Curriculum_Guide]] · [[Study Plan]]

---

## 📚 Core Concept

> **Core idea in one sentence:** Central force motion describes particle trajectories under forces directed toward a fixed point — the foundation of orbital mechanics.

> **Geodesy Connection:** Satellite orbits under Earth's gravity; two-body problem in GNSS orbit determination.

---

## 🧮 Key Equations

$$

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F} = f(r)\hat{r}
\end{equation}
\text{(Central force)}

$$

$$

\begin{equation}
V_{\text{eff}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{L^2}{2mr^2}
\end{equation}
\text{(Effective potential)}

$$

$$

\begin{equation}
r = \frac{p}{1 + e\cos\phi}, \quad p = \frac{L^2}{m|k|}
\end{equation}
\text{(Kepler orbit equation)}

$$

### Kepler's Laws

| Law | Statement | Equation |
|-----|-----------|----------|
| 1st | Orbits are conic sections | $r = \frac{a(1-e^2)}{1+e\cos\phi}$ |
| 2nd | Equal areas in equal times | $ \frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{L}{2m} = \text{const} $ |
| 3rd | Period squared ∝ semi-major axis cubed | $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$ |
