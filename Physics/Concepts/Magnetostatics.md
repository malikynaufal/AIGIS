# Magnetostatics (Magnetostatika)

> Biot-Savart law, Ampere's law, magnetic vector potential, magnetic materials, inductance

> **Part of:** [[Physics MOC]] · [[Physics_Curriculum_Guide]] · [[Study Plan]]

---

## 📚 Core Concept

> **Core idea in one sentence:** Magnetostatics describes the magnetic fields produced by steady (DC) electric currents, governed by the Biot-Savart law and Ampère's law, with no time-varying electric fields present.

> **Geodesy Connection:** Magnetic surveys for geophysical exploration; instruments like fluxgate and cesium magnetometers in geodesy; the Earth's main magnetic field originates from magnetohydrodynamic processes in the outer core.

---

## 🧮 Key Equations

### Biot-Savart Law (Hukum Biot-Savart)

The magnetic field $d\mathbf{B}$ produced by a current element $I\,d\boldsymbol{\ell}$ at position $\mathbf{r}$ is:

$$
d\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I\, d\boldsymbol{\ell} \times \hat{\mathbf{r}}}{r^2}
$$

For a complete circuit, the total field is obtained by integration:

$$
\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\oint \frac{I\, d\boldsymbol{\ell} \times \hat{\mathbf{r}}}{r^2}
$$

The magnetic permeability of free space is $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ T·m/A.

### Ampère's Law — Integral Form (Hukum Ampère)

$$
\oint_C \mathbf{B} \cdot d\boldsymbol{\ell} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}
$$

where $I_{\text{enc}}$ is the total current enclosed by the loop $C$.

### Ampère's Law — Differential Form

$$
\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J}
$$

where $\mathbf{J}$ is the current density (densitas arus). This is the magnetostatic equivalent of Gauss's law.

### Magnetic Field of Common Configurations

**Infinite straight wire** (Kawat lurus tak hingga):

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}
$$

**Solenoid** (Solenoida) with $n$ turns per unit length:

$$
B = \mu_0 n I \quad \text{(inside, ideal)}
$$

**Toroid** (Toroida) with $N$ total turns:

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r} \quad \text{(inside toroid)}
$$

**Circular loop** at center, radius $R$:

$$
B_{\text{center}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}
$$

**Helmholtz coils** (separated by $R$):

$$
B = \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{3/2} \frac{\mu_0 N I}{R}
$$

### Magnetic Vector Potential (Potensial Vektor Magnetik)

Since $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$, we can write:

$$
\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}
$$

For steady currents:

$$
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int \frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|} d^3r'
$$

### Gauss's Law for Magnetism

$$
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \oint_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{a} = 0
$$

This states there are no magnetic monopoles (monopola magnetik) — magnetic field lines always form closed loops.

### Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge

$$
\mathbf{F} = q\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}
$$

### Magnetic Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

$$
\mathbf{F} = I\int d\boldsymbol{\ell} \times \mathbf{B}
$$

### Magnetic Dipole Moment (Momen Dipol Magnetik)

A current loop of area $A$ with current $I$ has magnetic moment:

$$
\mathbf{m} = I A \hat{\mathbf{n}}
$$

The torque on a magnetic dipole in field $\mathbf{B}$:

$$
\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m} \times \mathbf{B}
$$

### Magnetic Materials (Bahan Magnetik)

For linear magnetic materials, the magnetization $\mathbf{M}$ relates to the applied field:

$$
\mathbf{M} = \chi_m \mathbf{H}
$$

where $\chi_m$ is the magnetic susceptibility and $\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\mathbf{B} - \mathbf{M}$. The modified Ampère's law becomes:

$$
\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_{\text{free}}
$$

The relative permeability is $\mu_r = 1 + \chi_m$, and $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mu_r \mathbf{H}$.

| Material Type | $\chi_m$ | $\mu_r$ | Examples |
|---------------|----------|---------|----------|
| Diamagnetic | $\chi_m < 0$, small | $\mu_r < 1$ | Copper, water, bismuth |
| Paramagnetic | $\chi_m > 0$, small | $\mu_r > 1$ | Aluminum, platinum |
| Ferromagnetic | $\chi_m \gg 1$ | $\mu_r \gg 1$ | Iron, nickel, cobalt |

### Inductance (Induktansi)

For a solenoid with $N$ turns and cross-section $A$:

$$
L = \frac{N\Phi_B}{I} = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{\ell}
$$

The energy stored in an inductor:

$$
U = \frac{1}{2}LI^2
$$

## 🧭 Physical Intuition & Mental Models

> **Visual analogy:** Magnetic field lines wrap around current-carrying wires like rubber bands — squeeze them (increase current) and the bands get tighter (stronger field).

> **Key insight:** The Biot-Savart law is to magnetostatics what Coulomb's law is to electrostatics — a direct integration of the field from all source elements. But unlike electric fields, magnetic fields always form closed loops (no monopoles).

> **Geodesy intuition:** Earth's magnetic field, generated by dynamo processes in the liquid outer core, is modeled using spherical harmonic expansion — the same mathematical tool used for the gravity field. The IGRF (International Geomagnetic Reference Field) model uses degree-13 spherical harmonics.

## 🧪 Worked Examples

### Example 1: Magnetic Field Inside a Solenoid

**Problem:** A solenoid has 500 turns, length 0.5 m, and carries 2.0 A. Find the magnetic field inside and the flux through a cross-section of radius 1.0 cm.

**Solution:**

The turn density is $n = N/\ell = 500/0.5 = 1000$ turns/m.

$$
B = \mu_0 n I = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 1000 \times 2.0 = 2.513 \times 10^{-3} \text{ T} \approx 2.51 \text{ mT}
$$

The flux through a circular cross-section of radius $r = 0.01$ m:

$$
\Phi_B = B \cdot \pi r^2 = 2.513 \times 10^{-3} \times \pi \times (0.01)^2 = 7.89 \times 10^{-7} \text{ Wb} \approx 0.789 \text{ μWb}
$$

---

### Example 2: Force Between Two Parallel Currents

**Problem:** Two parallel wires carry currents $I_1 = 5.0$ A and $I_2 = 3.0$ A in the same direction, separated by $d = 0.1$ m. Find the force per unit length.

**Solution:**

The magnetic field from wire 1 at the position of wire 2:

$$
B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d} = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5.0}{2\pi \times 0.1} = 1.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}
$$

The force per unit length on wire 2:

$$
\frac{F}{\ell} = I_2 B_1 = 3.0 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-5} = 3.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ N/m}
$$

Since the currents are in the same direction, the force is **attractive**. This is exactly how the Ampere was originally defined — the force between two parallel conductors.
