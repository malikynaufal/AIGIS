# FKD211304 — Optika

## Course Overview

Optics is the study of light — its propagation, interaction with matter, and the phenomena of reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference. This course covers both geometric optics (ray-based) and physical optics (wave-based), with direct applications to surveying instruments, laser technology, and atmospheric observation.

**Contact Hours:** 3 SKS (2 hours lecture + 1 hour tutorial per week)
**Prerequisites:** Fisika Dasar II, Kalkulus II, Elektromagnetik (co-requisite)
**Co-requisites:** Elektromagnetik

---

## 📋 Topics & Outline

### Unit 1: Geometric Optics (Weeks 1–5)

- **Fermat's principle:** light follows the path of least time

- **Snell's law:** n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)

- **Total internal reflection** and critical angle: θ_c = arcsin(n₂/n₁)

- **Reflection:** plane mirrors, curved mirrors (focal length, mirror equation)

- **Refraction** through single surfaces and lenses

- **Thin lens equation:** 1/f = 1/v - 1/u

- **Lens maker's equation:** 1/f = (n-1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂)

- **Optical instruments:**
 - Telescope: angular magnification M = f_objective/f_eyepiece
 - Microscope: total magnification
 - Human eye as optical instrument
 - Camera optics

### Unit 2: Physical Optics — Interference (Weeks 6–9)

- **Wave nature of light:** Huygens' principle, wavefront construction

- **Superposition principle** and coherent sources

- **Young's double slit:**
 ```
 Constructive: d sin θ = mλ (m = 0, ±1, ±2, ...)
 Destructive: d sin θ = (m+½)λ
 Fringe spacing: Δy = λL/d
 ```

- **Thin film interference** — reflection phase shifts
 - Soap bubbles, oil films, Newton's rings

- **Michelson interferometer:** measuring wavelengths, refractive indices

- **Fabry-Perot interferometer** and finesse

### Unit 3: Physical Optics — Diffraction (Weeks 10–13)

- **Single slit diffraction:**
 ```
 Destructive: a sin θ = mλ (m = ±1, ±2, ...)
 Central maximum width: Δθ = 2λ/a
 ```

- **Diffraction grating:** resolution R = mN (N = number of slits)
 - Grating equation: d sin θ = mλ

- **Rayleigh criterion:** θ_min = 1.22 λ/D (resolving power)

- **Diffraction by circular apertures:** Airy disk pattern

- **Fraunhofer vs. Fresnel diffraction**

### Unit 4: Polarization and Laser Physics (Weeks 14–16)

- **Polarization of light:** linear, circular, elliptical

- **Malus's law:** I = I₀ cos²θ

- **Brewster's angle:** θ_B = arctan(n₂/n₁)

- **Polarization by scattering** (why the sky is polarized)

- **Birefringence** and wave plates (quarter-wave, half-wave)

- **Laser principles:** stimulated emission, population inversion, cavity

- **Applications:** interferometric distance measurement, laser levels, LIDAR

## 🔬 Key Equations

```
Snell: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
Thin lens: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
Young's double slit: Δy = λL/d
Bragg: 2d sin θ = mλ
Single slit: a sin θ = mλ (minima)
Grating: d sin θ = mλ
Rayleigh: θ_min = 1.22 λ/D
Malus: I = I₀ cos²θ
Brewster: tan θ_B = n₂/n₁
```

## 🎯 Learning Outcomes

Upon completion, students will be able to:
1. Apply Snell's law and lens equations to solve optics problems
2. Analyze interference patterns from double slits, thin films, and interferometers
3. Compute diffraction patterns and resolving power of optical systems
4. Understand polarization phenomena and their applications
5. Explain laser operation and its use in precision measurement
6. Connect optics principles to geodetic instruments (theodolites, laser scanners, LIDAR)
