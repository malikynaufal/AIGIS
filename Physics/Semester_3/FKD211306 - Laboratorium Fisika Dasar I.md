# FKD211306 — Laboratorium Fisika Dasar I

## Course Overview

Hands-on laboratory experience in classical mechanics, reinforcing theoretical concepts through experimental measurement, data collection, and analysis. Students learn to use precision instruments, estimate uncertainties, and write professional lab reports — essential skills for any practicing physicist or engineer.

**Contact Hours:** 2 SKS (4 hours lab per week, biweekly)
**Prerequisites:** Fisika Dasar I
**Co-requisites:** Fisika Klasik

---

## 📋 Topics & Outline

### Experiment 1: Measurement and Error Analysis (Weeks 1–2)

- **Introduction to lab safety** and protocols

- Using Vernier calipers, micrometers, rulers

- **Systematic vs. random error** identification

- Statistical analysis of repeated measurements

- Uncertainty propagation for derived quantities

- **Lab report:** formal structure with error analysis

### Experiment 2: Uniformly Accelerated Motion (Weeks 3–4)

- Air-track frictionless motion or inclined plane

- Measuring position vs. time with motion sensors

- **Data analysis:** fitting quadratic to position data
  - Extracting acceleration: x(t) = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²

- Comparing measured g with accepted value (9.80665 m/s²)

### Experiment 3: Newton's Second Law (Weeks 5–6)

- Atwood machine or cart-on-track with pulley

- Measuring acceleration as function of force and mass

- **Verification of F = ma:** a ∝ F at constant m, a ∝ 1/m at constant F

- Systematic error: friction, string mass, pulley inertia

### Experiment 4: Conservation of Energy (Weeks 7–8)

- **Ballistic pendulum** or air-track spring system

- Measuring kinetic and potential energy interchange

- **Comparison:** mechanical energy vs. work done by friction

- Energy loss due to air resistance, friction

### Experiment 5: Conservation of Momentum (Weeks 9–10)

- Two-collision experiments on air track

- Elastic vs. inelastic collisions

- Measuring velocities before and after collision

- **Verification of momentum conservation:** Σp_before = Σp_after

### Experiment 6: Rotational Motion (Weeks 11–12)

- **Rotational inertia** measurement using pendulum or rotational apparatus

- **Parallel axis theorem** verification: I = I_cm + Md²

- Moment of inertia for solid disk, hollow cylinder, ring

### Experiment 7: Oscillations and Springs (Weeks 13–14)

- **Simple harmonic motion:** mass-spring system

- Measuring period T as function of mass: T = 2π√(m/k)

- **Determining spring constant** k from slope of T² vs. m plot

- Damped oscillations: measuring damping coefficient

### Experiment 8: Gravitational Acceleration (Weeks 15–16)

- **Free-fall timing** or **simple pendulum** method
  - Simple pendulum: g = 4π²L/T²

- Measuring g at UGM campus

- Comparison with theoretical and GPS-derived values

- Discussion: why g varies with latitude and altitude

## 🔬 Key Experimental Techniques

```
Error analysis:     σ_x = √(Σ(x_i - x̄)²/(N-1))
Propagation:        σ_f = √(Σ(∂f/∂x_i)² · σ_{x_i}²)
Best fit line:      y = mx + b  (linear regression)
  m = Σ(x_i - x̄)(y_i - ȳ) / Σ(x_i - x̄)²
  b = ȳ - m·x̄
Relative error:     δx = σ_x / |x̄| × 100%
```

## 🎯 Learning Outcomes

Upon completion, students will be able to:
1. Operate precision measurement instruments (motion sensors, scales, timers)
2. Identify and quantify systematic and random errors in experiments
3. Apply error propagation to computed results
4. Write professional laboratory reports with proper structure and citations
5. Extract physical constants from experimental data using fitting methods
6. Connect experimental results to theoretical predictions from Newton's laws
