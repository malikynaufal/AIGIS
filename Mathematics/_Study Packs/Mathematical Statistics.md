# Mathematical Statistics

## Overview

Mathematical statistics provides the theoretical foundations for statistical inference — how we draw conclusions about populations from data. This study pack covers sufficient statistics, the Cramér-Rao lower bound, maximum likelihood estimation, hypothesis testing theory, and the Neyman-Pearson lemma. These are essential for understanding error analysis in geodesy and for any rigorous statistical work.

**Prerequisites**: [[Probability Distributions]], [[Derivatives]], [[Linear Algebra]], [[Regression Least Squares]]

## 1. Statistical Models

### 1.1 Parametric Models

A statistical model is a family of probability distributions:

$$\mathcal{P} = \\{P_\theta : \theta \in \Theta\\} $ $

where $\Theta $ is the parameter space.

### 1.2 Examples

- Bernoulli( $ p $): $\Theta = [0,1] $

- Normal( $\mu, \sigma^2 $): $\Theta = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^+$

- Poisson( $\lambda $): $\Theta = (0, \infty) $### 1.3 Random Sample $ X_1, \ldots, X_n $ is a random sample from $ P_\theta $ if i.i.d. with $ X_i \sim P_\theta $.

---

## 2. Sufficient Statistics

### 2.1 Definition

$ T(X_1, \ldots, X_n) $ is a **sufficient statistic** for $\theta $ if the conditional distribution of $ (X_1, \ldots, X_n) $ given $  T $ does not depend on $\theta $.

### 2.2 Factorization Theorem

$ T $ is sufficient for $\theta $ $\\iff $ the likelihood factors as $ $ L(\theta; \mathbf{x}) = g(T(\mathbf{x}), \theta) \cdot h(\mathbf{x})$$

where $ g $ depends on $\theta $ only through $  T $, and $  h $ does not depend on $\theta $.

### 2.3 Examples

**Normal $ N(\mu, 1) $**: Sufficient for $\mu $ is $  T = \bar{X} $ (sample mean).

**Binomial $ (n, p) $**: Sufficient for $  p $ is $  T = \sum X_i $ (total successes).

### 2.4 Minimal Sufficiency $ T $ is **minimal sufficient** if it is a function of every other sufficient statistic.

### 2.5 Exponential Family

The exponential family has density

$ $ f(x|\theta) = h(x)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^k \eta_i(\theta)T_i(x) - A(\theta)\right) $ $**Theorem**: $\sum_{i=1}^k T_i(X_i) $ is sufficient for $\theta $.

## 3. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

### 3.1 Definition

$ $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ML}} = \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta} L(\theta; \mathbf{x}) = \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta} \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i|\theta
)

$$

Equivalently, maximize the log-likelihood: $ $

\\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \log f(x_i|\theta
)

$$

# ## 3.2 Score Function $ $ S(\theta) = \frac{\partial \\ell}{\partial \theta}$$

Set $ S(\theta) = 0 $ for MLE.

### 3.3 Worked Example: Normal MLE

Given $ X_1, \ldots, X_n \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2) $:

$ $\\ell(\mu,\sigma^2) = -\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi) - n\log\sigma - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2\frac{\partial \\ell}{\partial \mu} = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum(x_i-\mu) = 0 \\implies \hat{\mu} = \bar{X}\frac{\partial \\ell}{\partial \sigma^2} = -\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma^4}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2 = 0 \\implies \hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum(x_i-\bar{X})^2

$$

# ## 3.4 Invariance Property

If $\hat{\theta} $ is MLE of $\theta $ and $  g $ is a function, then $ g(\hat{\theta}) $ is MLE of $ g(\theta) $.

### 3.5 Consistency and Asymptotic Normality

Under regularity conditions:

$ $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{ML}} - \theta) \\xrightarrow{d} N(0, I(\theta)^{-1})

$$

where $ I(\theta) $ is the Fisher information.

## 4. Fisher Information

### 4.1 Definitio
n

$ $ I(\theta) = E\left[-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2}\log f(X|\theta)\right]$$

For sample of size $ n $: $ I_n(\theta) = nI(\theta) $.

### 4.2 Score Function Properties

$ $ E[S(\theta)] = 0\text{Var}(S(\theta)) = I(\theta)$$

# ## 4.3 Examples

**Normal $ N(\mu, \sigma^2) $**: $ I(\mu) = 1/\sigma^2 $, $ I(\sigma^2) = 1/(2\sigma^4) $

**Bernoulli( $ p $)**: $ I(p) = 1/[p(1-p)] $

**Poisson( $\lambda $)**: $ I(\lambda) = 1/\lambda $### 4.4 Information Inequality

For any unbiased estimator $\hat{\theta} $:

$ $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{I(\theta)} $$

---

## 5. Cramér-Rao Lower Bound

### 5.1 Statement

For any unbiased estimator $ T $ of $\tau(\theta) $:

$ $\text{Var}(T) \geq \frac{[\tau'(\theta)]^2}{I(\theta)} $$

# ## 5.2 Multivariate Case

For $\tau(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^d $, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^k $:

$ $\text{Var}(\mathbf{T}) \geq \nabla\tau^T I^{-1}(\theta)\nabla\tau

$$

# ## 5.3 Efficient Estimator

An estimator $\hat{\theta} $ is **efficient** if it achieves the Cramér-Rao bound $ $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) = \frac{1}{I(\theta)} $$

MLEs are asymptotically efficient: $\\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}_{\\text{ML}}) \to 1/[nI(\theta)] $.

### 5.4 Worked Example: Exponential Distribution

$ X_i \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda) $. $ I(\lambda) = 1/\lambda^2 $.

Cramér-Rao bound: $\text{Var}(\hat{\lambda}) \geq \lambda^2/n $.

The MLE $\hat{\lambda} = 1/\bar{X} $ is a biased estimator.
But $\bar{X}^{-1} $ achieves $\text{Var}(\hat{\lambda}) \to \lambda^2/n $ asymptotically.

The UMVUE (uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator) of $ 1/\lambda $ is $\bar{X} $, achieving CRLB with $\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \lambda^2/n $.

## 6. Hypothesis Testing Theory

### 6.1 Neyman-Pearson Lemma

For testing $ H_0: \theta=\theta_0 $ vs $ H_1: \theta=\theta_1 $:

The **most powerful** (MP) test rejects $ H_0 $ when

$ $\frac{L(\theta_0; \mathbf{x})}{L(\theta_1; \mathbf{x})} < k

$$

for some threshold $ k $ chosen to control $ P(\\text{Type I error}) = \alpha $.

### 6.2 Likelihood Ratio Test (LRT)

$ $\Lambda = \frac{\sup_{\theta \in \Theta_0}L(\theta)}{\sup_{\theta \in \Theta}L(\theta)} $$

Reject when $\Lambda < c $.

**Asymptotic**: $-2\log\Lambda \\xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{\dim(\Theta)-\dim(\Theta_0)} $### 6.3 Likelihood Equivalence

For $\sigma^2 $ known, testing $\mu = \mu_0 $:

The LRT is equivalent to the z-test:

$ $ 

Z = \frac{\bar{X}-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}
}

$$

# ## 6.4 Power Function $ $\beta(\theta) = P(\text{reject } H_0|\theta)

$$

| Type | Probability |
|------|------------|
| Type I error ( $\alpha $) | $ P(\text{reject } H_0|H_0 \text{ true}) $ |
| Type II error ( $\beta $) | $ P(\text{fail to reject}|H_1 \text{ true}) $ |
| Power ( $ 1-\beta $) | $ P(\text{reject }|H_1 \text{ true}) $ |

### 6.5 UMP Tests

A **uniformly most powerful** test is most powerful against all alternatives.

**Karlin-Rubin theorem**: For one-sided alternatives with monotone likelihood ratio, the UMP test is a one-sided test.

## 7. Bayesian Estimation

### 7.1 Prior, Likelihood, Posterio
r

$ $ p(\theta|\mathbf{x}) = \frac{L(\theta;\mathbf{x})p(\theta)}{\int L(\theta;\mathbf{x})p(\theta)\,d\theta}$$

# ## 7.2 Bayesian Point Estimation

| Loss | Estimator |
|------|-----------|
| Squared error $ L(\theta,a)=(\theta-a)^2 $ | Posterior mean $ E[\theta|\mathbf{x}] $ |
| Absolute error $ L(\theta,a)=|\theta-a|$ | Posterior median |
| 0-1 loss | Posterior mode (MAP) |

### 7.3 Credible Interval $ [a,b] $ is a $ 100(1-\alpha)\\%$ credible interval if $ P(a \leq \theta \leq b|\mathbf{x}) = 1-\alpha $.

### 7.4 Conjugate Priors

| Likelihood | Conjugate Prior | Posterior |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| Bernoulli | Beta | Beta |
| Normal( $\mu $, known) | Normal | Normal |
| Normal( $\sigma^2 $, known) | Normal | Normal |
| Poisson | Gamma | Gamma |

## 8. Interval Estimation

### 8.1 Confidence Interval

For $\bar{X} \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2/n) $:

$ $\bar{X} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} $ $### 8.2 $  t $-Interval (unknown $\sigma $)

$ $\bar{X} \pm t_{n-1,\alpha/2}\frac{S}{\sqrt{n}
}

$$

# ## 8.3 Wilson Score Interval (Binomial) $ $\frac{\hat{p}+\frac{z^2}{2n}\pm z\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}+\frac{z^2}{4n^2}}}{1+\frac{z^2}{n}}

$$

Better than the Wald interval for small $ n $ or extreme $\hat{p} $.

## 9. Practice Problems

### Problem 1
Find the MLE of $\theta $ for $ f(x) = \theta x^{\theta-1} $, $ 0 < x < 1 $, $\theta > 0 $.

**Solution**:

$ $\\ell(\theta) = n\log\theta + (\theta-1)\sum\log x_i\frac{d\\ell}{d\theta} = \frac{n}{\theta} + \sum\log x_i = 0\hat{\theta} = \frac{-n}{\sum\log x_i} $$

# ## Problem 2
Show that $\bar{X} $ is sufficient for $\mu $ when $ X_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2) $ with $\sigma^2 $ known.

**Solution**: By factorization theorem:

$ $ L(\mu) = \prod\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}e^{-(x_i-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)} = \frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2\right)\sum(x_i-\mu)^2 = \sum x_i^2 - 2\mu\sum x_i + n\mu^2 $$

$ g(\bar{x},\mu) = \exp(-n(\bar{x}-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)) $ and $ h(\mathbf{x}) = \exp(-\sum x_i^2/(2\sigma^2)) $.

$ T = \bar{X} $ is sufficient.$\\blacksquare $### Problem 3
Compute the Cramér-Rao lower bound for $ p $ in Bernoulli( $  p $).

**Solution**: $ I(p) = \frac{1}{p(1-p)} $.

For $\tau(p) = p $: CRLB $= \frac{1}{I(p)} = p(1-p) $.

The sample proportion $\hat{p}=\bar{X} $ achieves this: $\text{Var}(\bar{X})=p(1-p)/n $→ CRLB for $  n $ observations is $ p(1-p)/n $. ✓

### Problem 4
Derive the likelihood ratio test for $ H_0: \mu=\mu_0 $ vs $ H_1: \mu=\mu_1 $ for $ X_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2) $ with $\sigma^2 $ known.

**Solution**:

$ $\Lambda = \frac{L(\mu_0)}{L(\mu_1)} = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\left[\sum(x_i-\mu_0)^2-\sum(x_i-\mu_1)^2\right]\right)= \exp\left(-\frac{n(\bar{x}-\mu_0)^2-n(\bar{x}-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) = \exp\left(\frac{n(\mu_1-\mu_0)(2\bar{x}-\mu_0-\mu_1)}{2\sigma^2}\right)

$$

Reject $ H_0 $ when $\Lambda < k $:

- If $\mu_1 > \mu_0 $: reject when $\bar{X} > c $ (one-sided test)

- If $\mu_1 < \mu_0 $: reject when $\bar{X} < c $ This is the UMP test — equivalent to the z-test.$\\blacksquare $---

## 10. Applications in Geodesy

- **Outlier detection**: LRT for testing observation anomalies

- **Model selection**: AIC/BIC derived from MLE

- **Datum ambiguity**: Sufficient statistics in rank-deficient models

- **Bayesian methods**: Prior information in geodetic adjustment

- **Confidence regions**: Error ellipses from multivariate normal theory

- **Hypothesis testing**: $\chi^2 $ and F-tests for model adequacy
