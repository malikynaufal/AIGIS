# ∫ Integrals

Integration is the reverse of differentiation (antidifferentiation) and a tool for computing areas, volumes, work, and solving differential equations. This study pack covers integration techniques, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, applications, and numerical methods.

> **Indonesian glossary (Glosarium):** integral = integral, substitusi = substitution, parsial = partial, pecahan parsial = partial fractions, luas = area, volume = volume, numerik = numerical.

---

## 1. Core Definitions

### 1.1 Indefinite Integral (Antiderivative)

The indefinite integral of $f(x)$is the family of functions$F(x)$such that$F'(x) = f(x)$:
$$

\int f(x)\, dx = F(x) + C$$where$C$is the **constant of integration** (arbitrary constant).

### 1.2 Definite Integral

The definite integral of$f(x)$from$a$to$b$:
$$

\int_a^b f(x)\, dx = F(b) - F(a) = \left[F(x)\right]_a^b$$**Geometric meaning:** Signed area between the curve$y = f(x)$and the$x$-axis from $x = a$to$x = b$.

**Riemann integral (limit definition):**
$$

\int_a^b f(x)\, dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^*) \Delta x$$where$\Delta x = (b-a)/n$and$x_i^*$is a sample point in the$i$-th subinterval.

### 1.3 Improper Integrals

When the interval is infinite or $f$has a singularity:$$\int_a^\infty f(x)\, dx = \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_a^b f(x)\, dx$$The integral **converges** if the limit exists and is finite; otherwise it **diverges**.

**Example:**$\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\, dx = \lim_{b\to\infty}\left[-\frac{1}{x}\right]_1^b = 0 - (-1) = 1$---

## 2. Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

### 2.1 First Part (FTC-1)

If$f$is continuous on$[a,b]$and$F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\, dt$, then:
$$

F'(x) = f(x)$$**Meaning:** Differentiation undoes integration. The rate of change of the accumulated area is the integrand itself.

### 2.2 Second Part (FTC-2)

If$f$is continuous on$[a,b]$and$F$is any antiderivative of$f$:
$$

\int_a^b f(x)\, dx = F(b) - F(a)$$This connects the antiderivative to the definite integral and provides the practical method for evaluation.

## 4. Integration Techniques

### 4.1$u$-Substitution (Substitusi)

The counterpart of the chain rule. If $u = g(x)$, then $du = g'(x)\, dx$:
$$

\int f(g(x))\, g'(x)\, dx = \int f(u)\, du$$**Example:**$$\int 2x \cos(x^2)\, dx \quad \text{let } u = x^2, \, du = 2x\, dx
$$

$$
= \int \cos u\, du = \sin u + C = \sin(x^2) + C$$**Example:**$$\int \frac{x}{1+x^2}\, dx \quad \text{let } u = 1+x^2, \, du = 2x\, dx
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{du}{u} = \frac{1}{2}\ln|1+x^2| + C$$### 4.2 Integration by Parts

The counterpart of the product rule:$$\int u\, dv = uv - \int v\, du$$**LIATE rule** for choosing$u$: **L**ogarithmic, **I**nverse trig, **A**lgebraic, **T**rigonometric, **E**xponential (choose the leftmost as $u$).

**Example:**
$$\int x e^x\, dx \quad u = x,\; dv = e^x\, dx$$

$$
= xe^x - \int e^x\, dx = xe^x - e^x + C = e^x(x-1) + C$$**Example:**$$\int \ln x\, dx \quad u = \ln x,\; dv = dx
$$

$$
= x\ln x - \int x \cdot \frac{1}{x}\, dx = x\ln x - x + C$$**Tabular method** (for repeated integration by parts): create a table with alternating signs, derivatives of$u$, and integrals of $dv$.

### 4.3 Partial Fractions (Pecahan Parsial)

For rational functions $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$where$\deg P < \deg Q$:

**Distinct linear factors:**
$$

\frac{P(x)}{(x-a)(x-b)} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{x-b}$$**Repeated linear factor:**$$\frac{P(x)}{(x-a)^2} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{(x-a)^2}$$**Irreducible quadratic:**$$\frac{P(x)}{(x^2+bx+c)} = \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+bx+c}$$**Example:**$$\int \frac{5x+3}{(x+1)(x+2)}\, dx = \int\left(\frac{-2}{x+1} + \frac{7}{x+2}\right)dx = -2\ln|x+1| + 7\ln|x+2| + C$$### 4.4 Trigonometric Substitution (Substitusi Trigonometri)

| Form | Substitution | Identity |
|------|-------------|----------|
|$\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$|$x = a\sin\theta$|$1 - \sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta$|
|$\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}$|$x = a\tan\theta$|$1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$|
|$\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$|$x = a\sec\theta$|$\sec^2\theta - 1 = \tan^2\theta$|

**Example:**$$\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} \quad x = 2\sin\theta,\; dx = 2\cos\theta\, d\theta
$$

$$
= \int \frac{2\cos\theta}{2\cos\theta}\, d\theta = \theta + C = \arcsin\frac{x}{2} + C$$### 4.5 Trigonometric Integrals

**Powers of$\sin$and$\cos$:**

- $\int \sin^n x\, dx$: use reduction formulas or half-angle identities

- $\int \cos^n x\, dx$: similarly

- $\int \sin^m x \cos^n x\, dx$: if $m$or$n$is odd, substitute the other function; if both even, use half-angle formulas

**Powers of$\sec$and$\tan$:**

- $\int \sec^n x\, dx$: use reduction formula or integration by parts

- $\int \sec^2 x \tan^n x\, dx$: let $u = \tan x$### 4.6 Reduction Formulas$$\int \sin^n x\, dx = -\frac{\sin^{n-1}x\cos x}{n} + \frac{n-1}{n}\int \sin^{n-2}x\, dx
$$

$$
\int \cos^n x\, dx = \frac{\cos^{n-1}x\sin x}{n} + \frac{n-1}{n}\int \cos^{n-2}x\, dx$$---

## 5. Definite Integral Properties

| Property | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Bounds swap |$\int_a^b f\, dx = -\int_b^a f\, dx$|
| Linearity |$\int_a^b (cf + g)\, dx = c\int_a^b f\, dx + \int_a^b g\, dx$|
| Additivity |$\int_a^b f\, dx = \int_a^c f\, dx + \int_c^b f\, dx$|
| Dominance | If$f(x) \geq g(x)$on$[a,b]$, then $\int_a^b f\, dx \geq \int_a^b g\, dx$|
| Mean value |$\int_a^b f(x)\, dx = f(c)(b-a)$for some$c \in [a,b]$|

## 6. Applications

### 6.1 Area Between Curves$$A = \int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\, dx$$For functions of$y$: $A = \int_c^d |x_R(y) - x_L(y)|\, dy$### 6.2 Volume of Solids of Revolution

**Disk/Washer method** (revolve about$x$-axis):
$$

V = \pi \int_a^b [R(x)]^2\, dx$$**Washer method** (two functions):$$V = \pi \int_a^b \left([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2\right) dx$$**Shell method** (revolve about$y$-axis):
$$

V = 2\pi \int_a^b x \cdot f(x)\, dx$$### 6.3 Arc Length$$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2}\, dx$$**Application in geodesy:** Computing distances along curved surfaces (geodesic lines on the ellipsoid).

### 6.4 Work$$W = \int_a^b F(x)\, dx$$where$F(x)$is the force applied over distance.

### 6.5 Centroid$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{A}\int_a^b x[f(x) - g(x)]\, dx, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{1}{A}\int_a^b \frac{[f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2}{2}\, dx$$### 6.6 Probability$$P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b f(x)\, dx$$where$f(x)$is the probability density function.

### 6.7 Average Value of a Function$$f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\, dx$$---

## 7. Numerical Integration

When analytical integration is difficult or impossible, numerical methods approximate the integral.

### 7.1 Riemann Sum$$\int_a^b f(x)\, dx \approx \sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i) \Delta x$$### 7.2 Trapezoidal Rule$$\int_a^b f(x)\, dx \approx \frac{h}{2}\left[f(x_0) + 2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_i) + f(x_n)\right]$$where$h = (b-a)/n$. Error: $O(h^2)$.

### 7.3 Simpson's Rule (1/3 Rule)
$$

\int_a^b f(x)\, dx \approx \frac{h}{3}\left[f(x_0) + 4\sum_{i\text{ odd}} f(x_i) + 2\sum_{i\text{ even}} f(x_i) + f(x_n)\right]$$Requires$n$even. Error:$O(h^4)$.

### 7.4 Gaussian Quadrature

Uses optimally chosen nodes and weights for maximum accuracy:
$$

\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\, dx \approx \sum_{i=1}^n w_i f(x_i)$$| Points$n$| Nodes$x_i$| Weights$w_i$|
|------------|------------|---------------|
| 2 |$\pm 1/\sqrt{3}$| 1, 1 |
| 3 |$0, \pm\sqrt{3/5}$| 8/9, 5/9, 5/9 |

**Error:**$O(h^{2n})$— exponentially convergent for smooth functions.

### 7.5 Monte Carlo Integration

For high-dimensional integrals:$$\int_\Omega f(x)\, dx \approx \frac{|\Omega|}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N f(x_i)$$where$x_i$are random points in$\Omega$. Error: $O(1/\sqrt{N})$.
