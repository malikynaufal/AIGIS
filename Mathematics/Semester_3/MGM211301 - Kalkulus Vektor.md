# MGM211301 — Kalkulus Vektor (Vector Calculus)

## Course Information
- **Course Code:** MGM211301
- **Course Name:** Kalkulus Vektor (Vector Calculus)
- **Credit Hours (SKS):** 3
- **Semester:** 3
- **Prerequisites:** MGM211201 — Kalkulus II

## Course Description
Calculus of vector-valued functions and scalar fields in two and three dimensions. Covers derivatives, integrals, and the fundamental theorems of vector calculus including gradient, curl, divergence, and their applications to physics, engineering, and computational modeling.

## Key Topics

### Vector-Valued Functions
- Vectors in 2D and 3D: operations, dot product, cross product
- Vector-valued functions and parametric curves
- Arc length parameterization
- Curvature and the Frenet-Serret formulas
- Motion in space: velocity, acceleration, tangent and normal vectors

### Partial Differentiation
- Functions of several variables: domain, range, level curves and surfaces
- Limits and continuity in multiple variables
- Partial derivatives and their geometric meaning
- Total differential and differentiability
- Chain rule for multivariable functions
- Directional derivatives and the gradient vector
- Tangent planes and linear approximation

### Optimization in Several Variables
- Critical points: local maxima, minima, saddle points
- Second derivative test (Hessian matrix)
- Constrained optimization: Lagrange multipliers
- Method of Lagrange multipliers for two constraints
- Global optimization on compact sets (Extreme Value Theorem)
- Applications: economics, physics, geometry

### Vector Fields and Integration
- Vector fields: conservative and non-conservative
- Line integrals of scalar and vector fields
- Fundamental Theorem for Line Integrals
- Green's Theorem: circulation and flux forms
- Surface area and surface integrals
- Stokes' Theorem: generalization of Green's Theorem
- Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem)
- Triple integrals in cylindrical and spherical coordinates

### Applications
- Divergence, curl, and their physical interpretations
- Conservation laws and potential theory
- Fluid flow and velocity fields
- Electromagnetic field theory connections
- Computational modeling of vector fields

## Learning Outcomes
- Compute partial derivatives and use the chain rule for multivariable functions
- Perform optimization with and without constraints
- Evaluate line, surface, and volume integrals
- Apply Green's, Stokes', and Divergence theorems
- Analyze vector fields for conservative properties
- Model physical systems using vector calculus
