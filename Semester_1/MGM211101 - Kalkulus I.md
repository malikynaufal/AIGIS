# MGM211101 — Kalkulus I (Calculus I)

## Course Information
- **Course Code:** MGM211101
- **Course Name:** Kalkulus I (Calculus I)
- **Credit Hours (SKS):** 4
- **Semester:** 1
- **Prerequisites:** SMA-level Mathematics

## Course Description
Introduction to differential and integral calculus of a single variable. This course provides the foundational concepts of limits, continuity, differentiation, and their applications in real-world problem solving. Students develop rigorous mathematical reasoning through epsilon-delta definitions and develop computational fluency with derivatives and basic antiderivatives.

## Key Topics

### Limits and Continuity
- Intuitive concept of a limit and limit laws
- Epsilon-delta definition of a limit
- One-sided limits and infinite limits
- Continuity at a point and on an interval
- Intermediate Value Theorem and its applications
- Squeeze Theorem

### Differentiation
- Definition of the derivative as a limit
- Basic differentiation rules (power, product, quotient, chain rule)
- Derivatives of trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions
- Implicit differentiation
- Higher-order derivatives
- Logarithmic differentiation

### Applications of Derivatives
- Related rates problems
- Linear approximation and differentials
- Mean Value Theorem (Rolle's Theorem)
- Curve sketching (increasing/decreasing, concavity, inflection points)
- L'Hôpital's Rule for indeterminate forms
- Optimization problems (maxima and minima)
- Newton's method for root finding

### Introduction to Integration
- Antiderivatives and indefinite integrals
- Area under a curve (Riemann sums)
- Definite integrals and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
- Properties of definite integrals
- u-substitution technique

## Learning Outcomes
- Compute limits using algebraic techniques and limit laws
- Apply the epsilon-delta definition to prove limit statements
- Differentiate single-variable functions using standard rules
- Solve real-world optimization and related rates problems
- Evaluate definite integrals using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
- Apply integration techniques to compute areas and accumulated quantities
